Findings requiring action
------------------------
1. MEDIUM, documentation error: the source crosswalk says that changing the
initial e_1 action to e_1^0 makes the MDP nonabsorbing and its basis singular.
That is false for the currently implemented graph-derived matrix. Exact
rational elimination for n=3 and n=4 gives an invertible matrix and minimum
basic value 1. There is also a graph explanation: e_1^0 goes through s and
the c ladder to g_1, and F_1 has a positive-probability escape through h_1
and the a ladder to the sink. The reason to use e_1^1 is agreement with the
explicit counter initialization in Section 2.3 (p13) and the starting basis
in Section 4.4 (p31). Section 3.3 and Section 5.2 have abbreviated e_*^0
lists that conflict with those explicit descriptions. Remove the singularity
claim; retain the implemented initial policy.

2. MEDIUM, statement-strength gap: at audit start, realizesCube in
Def_friedmann_cunningham.lean:228 asserted the vertex and adjacency
correspondences but did not assert boundedness. Its midpoint and common-zero
support predicates alone describe a polyhedron; they do not exclude
unbounded rays. Although lp_valid separately targets boundedness, the
cube_realization and auso_lower_bound statements should include it themselves
when claiming a polytope realization. Suggested fix: add
FriedmannSimplex.bounded (program n) as a conjunct of realizesCube.
This is a strengthening of the specification, not evidence that this concrete
family is actually unbounded.

3. LOW, representation boundary: geometry is quantified over rational points
(Def_friedmann_cunningham.lean:216-234), whereas polytopes are normally
interpreted over the reals. For these rational standard-form coefficients,
the representation is mathematically adequate: every vertex is a rational
basic solution, and any nontrivial real feasible direction within a rational
face has a rational counterpart obtained from the rational kernel. Thus
vertex and edge tests agree after extension to the reals. This bridge is not
currently a Lean theorem. Document the rational formulation or add a general
rational-to-real transport result; do not advertise that transport as proved.

The transport argument can be made precise without an approximation premise.
Let S be the support of a feasible point. That point is extreme precisely
when the columns A_S are linearly independent: a dependence gives a kernel
direction, which can be multiplied by a sufficiently small scalar so both
signs retain nonnegative coordinates; conversely a midpoint decomposition
gives a nonzero supported kernel direction. Rational Gaussian elimination
shows that rational and real column independence agree. If the columns are
independent, the unique solution of A_S x_S=b is rational, so every real
vertex is rational. For two vertices, their minimal common face has support
S=support(x) union support(y), with (x+y)/2 positive on every coordinate of
S. Its affine dimension is |S|-rank(A_S), equally over either field. If that
dimension exceeds one, a rational kernel direction independent of y-x gives
a rational feasible point outside their segment. If it is one and the face
is bounded, the two extreme endpoints determine the whole face. This proves
equivalence of the implemented rational vertex/edge tests with the real
polytope skeleton once boundedness has been established. A rational uniform
coordinate bound also extends to the real feasible set: any violation would
admit a rational feasible violation, by a rational affine basis within its
support and rational parameter approximation preserving its strictly
positive coordinates and the violated bound.

Specification checks that passed
--------------------------------
Simplex layer, Def_friedmann_simplex.lean:
* Lines22-35 use actual selected-column basis matrices, require injective
  column selection and nonzero determinant, and impose nonnegative basic
  coordinates. The total inverse therefore does not create spurious legal
  simplex bases.
* Lines43-65 implement B^-1 A_j, c_j-c_B^T B^-1 A_j, and the minimum positive
  ratio leaving test. Both endpoints must be feasible. No iteration count is
  assumed in the LP or pivot data.
* Lines78-93 use rank-valued cursor storage consistently: last is a rank,
  and after entering j it becomes order(j). For the concrete family the
  column order is already the source order, so the permutation is identity.
* Lines97-101 require a feasible start, every pivot, and an optimal endpoint.
  The main theorem additionally fixes the start and asserts strict objective
  increase. Existence of a complete run is part of its conclusion, so there
  is no vacuous universal-over-empty-runs lower bound.

Concrete family, Def_friedmann_cunningham.lean:
* Lines29-68 enumerate the source decision nodes and the two outgoing actions
  per node, in its signed-action order (1), including the absent b_n.
* Lines73-76 choose legal source parameters and priority-induced rewards.
  For n>=3, N=2n+1>=2n and epsilon=N^(-2n-2)<=N^(-2n).
* Lines79-100 match the conservation equations, including the exceptional
  F_1 boundary and the terminal a_(n+1)=t omission.
* Lines102-109 include the three alias rewards whose separate source
  derivation is being audited by another agent. This semantic audit confirms
  their placement, not a literal identity with the displayed shorthand LP.
* Lines116-129 and313-325 use the explicit source counter initialization and
  the minimum initial cursor. Choosing e_1^1 is supported by the explicit
  source paragraphs identified above.
* Lines159-174 use the correct matrix orientation: columns store incoming
  flow, so policy values solve the transpose basis system. The Bellman
  improvement inequality includes immediate reward and expected continuation.
* The source graph and priorities in lines253-311 match Figure2, with b_1
  and c_1 identified with g_1. The terminal, F_1 boundary, and all forced
  one-successor vertices are retained. Assigning forced vertices to the
  opponent makes no semantic change because their moves are unique.

Multiplicity valuation:
Def_friedmann_multiplicity.lean:14-35 compares counts at the greatest
differing priority, preferring larger even and smaller odd counts. This
matches Section2.2. The final length comparison has the correct sign for
even/odd dominant cycles. ValueLE defined by the negation of the reversed
strict comparison includes the intended equivalence of equal count vectors.
The common playValue uses a genuinely recurring maximal vertex and its first
visit, not a transient vertex with a coinciding priority. The concrete graph
has a unique maximal-priority vertex on each directed cycle.

Geometry and companion targets:
* isVertex is the midpoint-extremality criterion, valid over rationals.
* polytopeAdjacent uses the minimal standard-form face determined by the
  common active zero coordinates. For distinct vertices it tests exactly
  whether that face is their segment. It is not an arbitrary graph field.
* cubeAdjacent is Hamming distance one; all nonempty cube faces are represented
  by partial Boolean assignments; isAUSO enforces every edge orientation,
  acyclicity, and exactly one sink on each such face.
* policy_pivot asks for an actual minimum-ratio pivot, not merely matching
  improving labels. The LP-to-MDP identity has the correct transpose.
* All nine theorem files import definitions only and contain one intentional
  sorry each. There is no hidden proved lower-bound assumption. Their proofs
  are still open; this audit is not proof completion.

Independent execution check
--------------------------
Added notes/cunningham_semantic_probe.py. Its parity evaluator builds Figure2
directly by named vertices, enumerates all opponent choices at F vertices,
evaluates the actual eventual cycle of each deterministic play, and takes
the minimum multiplicity valuation. It does not use the LP's reward formula
or linear equations. It compares the entire selected-action sequence against
the exact-rational simplex implementation, not merely the number of steps.

Each opponent play checked had a unique maximum-priority cycle vertex, and
every simplex pivot had a unique minimum-ratio leaving row. The n=3 count
also agrees with the source's explicit worked example (p15). These are
independent finite checks; the arbitrary-n binary-counter argument and
source-equivalence theorem still require proofs.

Conclusion
----------
No false or vacuous core lower-bound statement was found in this review.
The initial-policy rationale needs correction, and polytope boundedness
should be included in the realization predicate. Subject to those repairs,
the inspected definitions form a faithful rational reconstruction of the
source graph, algorithms, and mathematical targets. This conclusion is an
independent mathematical/code review, not a Lean proof of the open claims.
