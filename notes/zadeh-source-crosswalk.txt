Items and source pointers

Definitions.Def_friedmann_zadeh
* Centre / Node / priority / successors: journal Section 3, Table 1; full
  version Table 3.1, pages 6–7. Levels are zero-based in Lean. Centre (i,j)
  exists precisely when i+j<n; absent top centres cannot enter the vertex type.
* initial: journal Definition 5, full Definition 6.1, page 13.
* closed / bitState / Canonical: journal Definitions 2–4. Missing centres are
  explicitly not closed. Source statements mentioning both top centres are
  interpreted only over centres actually present in the graph.
* edgeGroup / internalBefore / before / counterChoice: journal Section 6.2,
  Definition 7; full Section 6.2, Definition 6.4, pages 14–16.
  Includes all seven groups, context-specific escape ordering, upper-selector
  ordering, five cycle-edge precedence criteria, and initial zero-count
  exceptional choice. A polynomial-size predecessor-count formula chooses a
  deterministic total completion. tie_order is an explicit correctness target.
* pgPlayScore: ordinary priority-multiplicity valuation order, represented by
  integer radix. The source gives repeated priorities, so applying an injective
  vertex-set comparator literally would be ill-defined. The valuation target
  proves score-order equivalence to the standard multiplicity comparator.
* pgValue: minimum of actual legal opponent play scores, with a zero fallback
  only for an empty opponent set. Source graph totality makes that branch
  unreachable; sink additionally supplies a common minimizing opponent.
* transition / mdpReward / policyMatrix: journal Section 4 and Figure 5; full
  Section 4, Figure 4.1. Figure 5 labels g, s and h with nonzero rewards and
  leaves b,d,e,F unlabelled. Low-priority terms are also absent from the MDP
  reduced-valuation expressions in full Appendix A.2 (Table A.3).
  IMPORTANT AUDIT CONVENTION: deterministic h nodes are retained as one-action
  reward states. The source prose describes these as random with probability
  one while also assigning their outgoing reward in the figure. Retaining h
  makes its high reward explicit. Dropping h's reward is not equivalent.
* proper: nonsingularity of the Bellman system with terminal boundary V(t)=0.
  The MDP main and run definitions do not permit division by zero inverses.
* inducedLP: Section 5 occupancy-flow LP; deterministic h vertices retained.
  It has 14n−6 rows and 26n−12 columns. This is an explicit equivalent
  deterministic-vertex expansion, rather than an assertion that those are
  the exact dimensions of a displayed source formula after elimination.
* inducedTie: reconstruct the policy from the current basis, translate column
  occurrence counts to action-edge counts, then apply the same explicit source
  tie rule in the MDP context. No arbitrary function is supplied by the solver.

Theorems
* size: graph Table 1; exact retained-vertex LP counts are a bookkeeping lemma.
* valuation: Avis–Friedmann Section 2.2; integer representation lemma.
* sink: journal Lemma 1, full Lemma 6.3, parity part.
* tie_order: journal Section 6.2 and Definition 7; explicit linear completion.
* mdp_valid: Section 4 / Lemma 1 initialization and transition normalization.
* counter_coverage: canonical enumeration consequence of journal Lemma 6;
  not a claim to include all technical occurrence-record properties in it.
* source_realization: journal Definition 7 / Section 6.3. The executable rule
  obtains t→ by comparing values of g1 and b2, while the source phrases t→
  through the number b at the start of a transition. The theorem supplies a
  partition of an actual complete run into canonical transitions and proves
  that every chosen switch obeys the source b-indexed choice. Thus this
  convention is a named proof obligation, not a hidden assertion of identity.
* lp_transfer: Section 5. Every policy switch, occurrence count and pivot is
  preserved. Actual inverse matrices, feasibility, reduced costs and ratio
  tests come from the shared FriedmannSimplex model. No correspondence is
  assumed as a theorem hypothesis.
* goal: journal Theorem 4 and Section 5; full Theorem 6.18. Each of PG, MDP,
  LP has a complete run, and every admitted complete run needs at least 2^n
  improving operations. The fixed graph and rule appear in definitions.

Parameter and measurement conventions
* n>0, N>=7n, rational 0<epsilon<N^(-(2n+11)).
* All initial occurrence counts are zero. Selecting an edge increases its
  counter by exactly one; original basic variables do not receive fictitious
  historical entries. LP counts refer to entering variable columns.
* T counts successful improving switches/pivots. There are T+1 displayed
  strategy states including the initial configuration.
* General bit complexity of arbitrary LP input and formal machine-time
  complexity of the tie implementation are not separate theorem targets.
  Graph cardinalities, probabilities, exact coefficients, and count updates
  are explicit and may be inspected independently.
* No global polytope boundedness is assumed: occupancy feasible sets can be
  unbounded even when the optimization objective attains a finite optimum.

Exploratory verification (not Lean certificates)
notes/zadeh_explore.py runs exact small instances. Exhaustive opponent-policy
evaluation agrees with an independent sink shortest-path evaluation for n<=3.
PG n=1:5 switches, n=2:43, n=3:159, n=4:465. These correspond to 160/466
displayed states for n=3/4, matching the source's published animation sizes.
Exact rational Bellman solves with N=7n and epsilon=1/(N^(2n+11)+1) give MDP
n=1:5 switches, n=2:43, n=3:161. Every matrix encountered is nonsingular.
These finite runs support encoding review; they establish no general theorem.

Historical source distinction
The 2011 IPCO paper is Friedmann alone. The 2019 Disser–Hopp paper repairs
three issues in that earlier construction while retaining the lower bound.
This mission uses the stronger joint 2023 paper and its full proof.
The construction and the fixed tie rule do not establish the lower bound
uniformly over every possible tie-breaking strategy.
