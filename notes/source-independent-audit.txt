1. GENERAL ALGEBRAIC DERIVATION

Let G_i=(-N)^(2i-1), H_i=(-N)^(2i), and S=1. These are the rewards in the
MDP, not its priority labels. Section 3.2 assigns a reward (-N)^Omega(v) to
each outgoing edge of a priority-bearing vertex. Figure 7 gives the MDP
priorities 2i-1, 2i, and 0. Thus the package's rewardG/rewardH and reward 1
at s are exactly the source MDP reward convention; no unproved shift from
the parity game's distinct priority scale is being silently applied.

Fix n>=3 and 0<epsilon<1. Write q=(1-epsilon)/2 and identify a_(n+1) with
the absorbing sink t, b_1 and c_1 with g_1. Given any continuation values
V at the decision vertices and V(t)=0, elimination of the intermediate
vertices gives the following identities by substitution:

  V(h_i) = H_i + V(a_(i+1)),
  V(s)   = 1 + V(c_n),
  V(F_i) = epsilon*(H_i+V(a_(i+1))) + q*V(d_i)+q*V(e_i), i>=2,
  V(F_1) = epsilon*(H_1+V(a_2)) + (1-epsilon)*V(e_1),
  V(g_i) = G_i + V(F_i).

Here V(F_i) denotes the expected continuation through the random vertex,
not a policy choice at that vertex. The F_1 equation follows Figure 8 and
the e_1 conservation equation. Applying the interior q probability to its
only non-h successor would lose half the remaining probability mass.

The following exhaustive action table results. An entry (v,p) means the
next original decision vertex is v with probability p; sink mass is omitted.
The reward is accumulated only until that next decision vertex or sink.

  action                         reward           next-decision distribution
  a_i^0, i<n                    0                (a_(i+1),1)
  a_n^0                         0                none: sink with probability 1
  b_i^0, i>2                    0                (b_(i-1),1)
  c_i^0, i>2                    0                (c_(i-1),1)
  d_i^0, i>2                    0                (b_(i-1),1)
  b_2^0,c_2^0,d_2^0             G_1+epsilon*H_1  (a_2,epsilon),(e_1,1-epsilon)
  a_i^1,b_i^1,c_i^1             G_i+epsilon*H_i  (a_(i+1),epsilon),(d_i,q),(e_i,q)
  d_i^1,e_i^1, i>=2             epsilon*H_i      (a_(i+1),epsilon),(d_i,q),(e_i,q)
  e_1^1                        epsilon*H_1      (a_2,epsilon),(e_1,1-epsilon)
  e_i^0                        1                (c_n,1)

Only existing actions are included: a,c,d levels 2..n; b levels 2..n-1;
e levels 1..n. For a_(n+1) entries the corresponding epsilon mass reaches
the sink, so the final listed row is substochastic after sink deletion.

This table covers every action and every endpoint case for arbitrary n>=3.
It yields the LP without invoking any long-run or lower-bound argument:
for each original decision row u, set A[u,j]=1_{u=source(j)}-P(j,u), b[u]=1,
and c[j]=expected_reward(j). Expanding this formula produces exactly the
seven classes of conservation equations displayed in Section 4.4, including
the a_2, e_1 and c_n boundary rows. This is also the coefficient formula
implemented in Def_friedmann_cunningham.lean after its row/column naming.
One unit is injected at each original decision row, as in the displayed
Section 4.4 LP; no extra unit is introduced at eliminated intermediate nodes.

2. WHY THE THREE OBJECTIVE COEFFICIENTS ARE REQUIRED

The actions b_2^0 and d_2^0 lead to b_1, and c_2^0 leads to c_1. All three
targets are g_1. Their zero-labelled actions therefore have reward

  R = G_1 + epsilon*H_1 = -N + epsilon*N^2,

whereas zero-labelled b,c,d actions at higher levels have zero immediate
reward before the next decision vertex. The graph-derived objective thus
adds R*(b_2^0+c_2^0+d_2^0) to the displayed reward-interpreted objective.
In particular, reward here is a signed quantity: the package's parameter
choice makes R negative, so it is a penalty rather than a positive bonus.

There are TWO separate source-display issues:
  (a) the printed Section 4.4 expression uses Omega rather than (-N)^Omega;
  (b) even after interpreting Omega there as priority-induced rewards, it
      gives coefficient zero to these three boundary actions.
The existing 43-versus-794 pivot comparison changes only (b). It is NOT a
comparison with a wholly literal priority-valued objective.

This is an algebraic deduction from the graph and outgoing-edge rewards.
It does not depend on choosing terms to fit observed pivot counts. Keeping
the constraints while omitting R changes the resulting optimization problem.

3. THE DIFFERENCE IS NOT A CONSTANT ON THE FEASIBLE POLYTOPE

It is legitimate to ask whether the omitted expression can be absorbed into
the constraints, leaving all objective comparisons unchanged. An exact
counterexample excludes that explanation.

Set n=3, N=7, epsilon=1/5764801. Two strictly positive feasible policy bases
have alias flow S=b_2^0+c_2^0+d_2^0 equal to:

  explicit Section 4.4 initial basis: S=20176805/2882401;
  same policy with only b_2 changed:  S=20176804/2882401.

As R is nonzero, R*S differs on these two feasible points. Hence it cannot
equal a constant, nor can its coefficient vector be A^T*y for any y (which
would give the constant y^T*b on Ax=b). This does not by itself prove every
pairwise objective ordering changes; it proves the proposed constraint-based
constant-offset explanation is unavailable.

notes/cunningham_objective_witness.py stores all coordinates explicitly and
checks them using direct substitution into the ten source equations. It
does not import the package's coefficient generator or pivot simulator.
It verifies strict positivity and nonzero basis determinants with exact
rational arithmetic. Its output is notes/cunningham-objective-witness.json.
These finite certificates are independently checkable mathematical witnesses,
not a universal Lean proof of the graph-to-LP translation.

4. INITIAL-STATE DISCREPANCY AND A CORRECTION TO OUR EARLIER NOTES

Sections 2.3 and 4.4 explicitly prescribe e_1^1, with e_i^0 for i>1. This
matches initialBit and both the game and LP start in the package. Sections
3.3 and 5.2 instead give the shorthand all-e_i^0. The latter is not the same
starting state and must not be described as textually identical.

The previous local note claiming the all-e_i^0 initial MDP policy was
nonabsorbing or singular is FALSE. The witness script gives a strictly
positive nonsingular basis for that alternative at n=3. More generally,
the graph supplies a positive-probability route through h_1,a_2,...,a_n,t;
there is no absorbing nonterminal class in that initial policy.

The difference is a one-step prefix. In the all-e_i^0 policy all a_i^0
actions continue up to the sink, so V(a_i)=0. The c ladder continues to g_1.
Consequently:

  V(e_1) = 1 + V(c_n) = 1 + G_1 + V(F_1).

Changing only e_1 to its action 1 has improvement V(F_1)-V(e_1)=N-1>0.
Since e_1^1 is first in ordering (1), Cunningham chooses it immediately.
This reaches exactly the package's initial policy and cursor. Thus the
all-e0 convention gives one preliminary switch followed by the same run.

The package's simplex target matches the explicit start of Theorem 7 in
Section 4.4. The parity target matches Section 2.3/Theorem 4. The MDP and
AUSO companion targets use this common normalized start: they are not
literal reproductions of the conflicting shorthand in Sections 3.3 and
5.2. Removing a prefix from a statement 'at least 2^n' does not formally
preserve that exact bound by itself. Their intended justification is the
paper's transfer of the full Section 2.3 counter run, not the arithmetic
argument 'subtract one from Theorem 6'. This distinction must remain clear
in source mapping and future proofs.

5. AUDIT CONCLUSION

The graph-derived three coefficients are justified by the source equations;
they are not optional terms or unexplained numerical repairs. The boundary
transition and chosen reward exponents agree with the explicit graph. The
paper's compact displayed objective is not identical to that graph-derived
LP, even after interpreting its priority symbols as rewards.

The statement package may accurately be described as formalizing the
graph-derived construction with explicitly reconciled boundary and initial
conventions. It must not be described as a literal transcription of every
displayed formula. The earlier singular-initial-policy rationale must be
removed. No author endorsement or published erratum has been established.
This report settles the source-level algebra for the disputed reward term;
the universal Lean correspondence and exponential lower-bound proofs are
separate obligations, with their status reported by the supervising audit.
