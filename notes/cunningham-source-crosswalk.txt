Avis and Friedmann Cunningham mission source crosswalk
=====================================================

Paper: David Avis and Oliver Friedmann, An exponential lower bound for
Cunningham's rule, Mathematical Programming 161, 271–305 (2017).
Published online 11 April 2016. DOI 10.1007/s10107-016-1008-4.
Primary working text: author's accepted 35-page manuscript:
https://oliverfriedmann.com/downloads/papers/cunningham_exponential_lower_bound.pdf
The 2013 arXiv version has different theorem numbers:
https://arxiv.org/html/1305.3944v1

Construction and conventions
----------------------------
The formal model uses rational coefficients, standard-form maximization,
five kinds of decision states, and two actions per state. There are exactly
5(n−1) rows and 10(n−1) columns; every target requires n≥3. Columns are
numbered in the exact cyclic order (1), including separate names for the
two directions at each decision state. Therefore the generic rule uses
the identity rank permutation. The scan begins with the minimum rank and
resumes at the last entering column; that column is then basic and is
ineligible to re-enter immediately.

The chosen parameters N=2n+1 and epsilon=N^(−2n−2) satisfy the source's
requirements N≥2n and 0<epsilon≤N^(−2n). The source's priority-induced
rewards are g_i=(-N)^(2i−1), h_i=(-N)^(2i), and s=1.

IMPORTANT SOURCE DISPLAY DISCREPANCY
------------------------------------
program n is derived from Figure 7 and the general transformation in
Sections 4.2–4.3. It must not be described as a literal transcription of
the displayed LP in Section 4.4. The source identifies b_1=c_1=g_1.
Eliminating this random vertex gives each of the actions b_2^0,c_2^0,d_2^0
the immediate reward g_1+epsilon*h_1 as well as its displayed transition
probabilities. Those three reward contributions are retained in
objectiveCoefficient. The displayed Section 4.4 objective, read with
nonexistent variables set to zero as instructed there, omits them; it
also writes priority notation Omega where the preceding construction
uses priority-induced rewards. This package uses the graph-derived
reward interpretation explicitly and records the difference here.

The source's Section 4.4 initial basis includes e_1^1 and e_i^0 for i>1.
Section 2.3 also explicitly starts the counter at 00...001 with e_1^1.
Some shorthand descriptions in Sections 3.3 and 5.2 instead list e_*^0.
That alternative is absorbing and has a nonsingular basis: our earlier
claim to the contrary was incorrect. From that alternative, the first cyclic
improvement switches e_1 to action 1, with advantage N-1>0. The package uses
the explicit Sections 2.3 and 4.4 starting state, after that optional prefix.

At the bottom level, F_1 has no d_1 successor. The boundary transition
is epsilon to h_1 and 1−epsilon to e_1, as depicted in Figure 8 and
reflected in the displayed e_1 conservation equation. The generic
Figure 7 interior-row probability 0.5*(1−epsilon) must not be copied
to this missing-successor boundary without that adjustment.

The source-faithfulness audit is complete; see cunningham-audit-summary.txt
and source-independent-audit.txt in this directory. The three contributions
follow algebraically from the graph, and a Lean certificate connects the
local elimination to the implemented coefficients for all n. This is not a
claim that an erratum has been published or that the lower bound is proved.

Exact rational sanity checks
----------------------------
notes/cunningham_numeric.py independently solves the linear systems and
implements reduced-cost entering choices, minimum-ratio leaving choices,
and the prescribed cyclic order. With the graph-derived rewards it finds:

 n   rows  columns  pivots  2^n
 3    10      20       36     8
 4    15      30      118    16
 5    20      40      320    32
 6    25      50      794    64

Every checked basis had strictly positive basic values and every pivot
strictly increased the objective. This is discovery evidence, not a
Lean proof and not a proof of any asymptotic lower bound.

With the three alias reward contributions omitted, the reward-based
transcription gives only 19,27,35,43 pivots respectively and fails the
n=6 lower bound. This check caught the missing alias contributions before
the formal capstone was written.
This comparison interprets the displayed priority symbols as rewards in
both variants; it changes only the three alias contributions. Independent
feasible witnesses show the difference is not a constant objective offset.

Declaration crosswalk
---------------------
Definitions.Def_friedmann_simplex supplies actual standard-form simplex
semantics: nonsingular basis matrices, feasible basic solutions, reduced
costs, minimum-ratio leaving rows, basis update, and cyclic entering rule.
No runtime or correctness claim is included as a field of LP data.

Definitions.Def_friedmann_multiplicity supplies the occurrence-count
valuation from Section 2.2, including equivalent paths through different
vertices with identical counts. It imports the common Game/play layer
but does not use the injective-priority comparison or WellFormed predicate.

Definitions.Def_friedmann_cunningham supplies the graph-derived family,
the exact order (1), prescribed initial basis, eliminated-state MDP,
policy/BFS correspondence interfaces, actual polytope-graph conditions over
rational points, cube faces, AUSO and signed-direction cyclic walk.

counterstrategy: Lemma 1 on the explicit repeated-priority family, including
a single counterstrategy simultaneously optimal from every starting vertex.

parity_lower_bound: Theorem 4, using the explicit Figure 2 graph,
prescribed initial strategy, order (1), and multiplicity comparison.

lp_valid: Section 4.2 policy/BFS correspondence and bounded-family
construction, made explicit as boundedness and positive policy bases.

mdp_valid: Figure 7 and Lemma 8 (absorbing Markov chains), represented by
substochastic transition rows and invertible transient systems after
the random vertices have been eliminated. The invertibility statement
does not by itself assert every probabilistic detail of Lemma 8.

policy_pivot: Sections 4.2–4.3, including Lemma 11. Objective equals the
sum of decision-state values; improving actions exactly match positive
reduced costs, and switching one action is a minimum-ratio simplex pivot.

mdp_lower_bound: Theorem 6 in the accepted manuscript (Theorem 3.1 in
arXiv v1), on the equivalent decision-state MDP. Complete run from the
prescribed initial policy has at least 2^n switches.

simplex_lower_bound: Theorem 7 (Theorem 4.1 in arXiv v1), for the
graph-derived LP and prescribed order/starting basis. It asserts a
complete run with at least 2^n pivots and strict increase at every step.

cube_realization: Lemma 12. The feasible set is explicitly bounded. Policies
map bijectively onto actual extreme
points; one-bit changes correspond exactly to edges of the feasible
polytope. A perturbed rational objective may orient formerly tied edges,
must retain every original strict improvement, and defines an AUSO.

auso_lower_bound: Theorem 8 (Theorem 5.1 in arXiv v1). The realized cube
has dimension 5(n−1), rather than n. Its signed-direction cyclic walk
has at least 2^n edges and terminates at its unique sink. Both orientation
and realization are explicit mathematical conditions, not assumed fields.

The MDP and AUSO statements use the explicit Sections 2.3/4.4 initial
counter state. Their source mapping invokes the full counter-run transfer,
not subtraction of one from the bound associated with shorthand all-e-zero
starts. The semantic audit also explains the rational-to-real polytope
representation; that bridge is not yet a checked Lean transport theorem.

The parity-game companion Theorem 4 uses repeated priorities. The source-
specific model retains them rather than postulating a priority-refinement
reduction. Its cycle valuation uses the common finite-orbit play layer;
all set/valuation comparisons are the occurrence-count definitions.

All theorem declarations are OPEN mission targets with sorry bodies.
Definitions must compile without sorry, custom axioms or unchecked
algorithm interfaces. The package does not claim the lower bounds proved.
