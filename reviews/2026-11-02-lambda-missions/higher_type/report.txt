FINDING H1 — BLOCKING: THEOREM 4.3 / COROLLARY 4.4 IS FALSE
Target: BNS2000.theorem_4_3_and_4_4
Published theorem ID: 5e24e9ec-f0f3-47bf-a0d2-46a45e19ddf5
Theorem code: code/04_BNS2000_theorem_4_3_and_4_4.lean:15
Defective dependency: code/01_BNS2000_Terms.lean:316 (also 325, 339, 352)

The evaluator represents the trailing elimination spine with rs : List Term and Term.appMany. This permits ordinary term arguments only; it cannot represent a trailing kappa. In particular, take
  bad := kappa (kappa (bang (bang (const zero))))
which is the source notation (!!0) kappa kappa.
This is a closed, R-free RS term of ground type iota. It reduces to zero by two contextual reduction steps. Nevertheless it has NO NFEval derivation, for any output, environment, step count, register count, or maximum length. The variable-kappa rule only starts from a variable. The bang-kappa rule requires an immediate kappa(bang s) followed by ordinary applications. Neither applies to bad, whose outer kappa contains another kappa.

Counterexample.lean proves bad_RS, bad_typed, bad_reduces_zero, and bad_no_eval. It repeats the exact submitted theorem type in Submitted4_3and4_4 and proves its negation in refute_submitted_4_3_and_4_4 (line 136). It imports only the definition modules, not the open target theorem. Kernel axioms reported: propext and Quot.sound; no sorry, custom axiom, or native_decide.

Fix: represent elimination spines by a datatype including both term arguments and kappa, or supply complete operational rules for kappa over all possible heads, with revised resource accounting. Retain this example as a regression obligation and prove evaluator completeness for the actual RS grammar before restating the bound. A cosmetic change to the theorem description cannot fix it.

FINDING H2 — BLOCKING FOUNDATION: TermEquiv IS NOT TRANSITIVE
Definition ID: 524b25b3-0657-4c05-9ec3-3206a0778e9e (BNS2000_Syntax)
Code: code/00_BNS2000_Syntax.lean:263 (substitution), :439 (TermEquiv)
Affected users include Theorem 4.5 and Theorem 5.1.

The syntax uses raw named binders, capture-avoiding substitution chooses a fresh numeric identifier from the current syntax, and TermEquiv requires a literally identical common normal form. There is no alpha-equivalence quotient, canonicalization, or renaming conversion. Reduction order therefore changes the chosen binder names.

Concrete well-typed example, with x=id1, y=id0, z=id10, all of type iota:
  (lambda x. lambda y. x) ((lambda z. y) 0).
Outer beta first yields the normal form lambda id11. y.
Inner beta first yields the normal form lambda id2. y.
The two normal forms are alpha-equivalent but are unequal Lean Term values and cannot reduce further. Consequently TermEquiv relates the first normal form to the source and the source to the second normal form, but fails to relate the two normal forms.

Counterexample.lean proves two_distinct_normal_forms and equivalence_not_transitive, using only the definition modules and axioms propext and Quot.sound. These are actual proofs, not evaluations over a sample. This finding does not by itself refute every theorem mentioning TermEquiv; it shows the foundational object does not provide the equivalence/unique-normal-form semantics used in the paper.

Fix: use de Bruijn or locally nameless syntax, or define and respect alpha equivalence throughout substitution, reduction, normalization, affination, sharing, and term equivalence. Merely adding an alpha clause to the current relation needs a full coherence audit of those dependent definitions. New definitions and downstream theorem items are required.

FINDING H3 — HIGH: THEOREMS 4.5 AND 5.1 OMIT THE CONSTRUCTION COST
Targets:
  BNS2000.theorem_4_5 — 5eb076ec-d2e4-4bc5-9e1f-e3de47f2bb7e
  BNS2000.theorem_5_1 — 6697cd6f-3349-437a-a835-5aded351f76f
Code: code/05_BNS2000_theorem_4_5.lean:12 and code/06_BNS2000_theorem_5_1.lean:19

Theorem 4.5 only says an equivalent R-free term exists with polynomial length. No elimination execution or its cost appears. Theorem 5.1 existentially selects an equivalent R-free term u and bounds NFEval applied to u; it never bounds the cost of obtaining u from the original term and numeric inputs. ReducesTo/TermEquiv do not carry a step bound.

Thus an arbitrarily expensive computation may be hidden in the choice of u. Choosing u to be the already-computed output numeral illustrates why cheaply evaluating u is insufficient to bound the computation of the original input. The statements may be true consequences, but they are weaker than the claimed polynomial-time normalization results. This is not a false-theorem claim.

Fix: add a costed recursor-elimination relation or an actual program and a simulation theorem, then include construction plus evaluation costs in the final bound. The current algebraic RA iff BC goal is a separate possible mission endpoint, but its existence does not repair these advertised complexity milestones. If polynomial time is defined through BC, say so explicitly and add a formal machine-time bridge if the mission claims one.
