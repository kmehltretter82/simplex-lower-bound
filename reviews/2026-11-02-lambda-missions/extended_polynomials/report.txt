EP-1 — Medium: composition and iteration helpers need a fresh-binder contract.
Item: Schwichtenberg1976_Lambda
Published definition ID: 1b8fdb07-70ed-450d-a3a9-1ff2a939563f
Locations: code/00_Schwichtenberg1976_Lambda.lean:231–245

compTerm accepts arbitrary terms t,s, then binds the supplied/default variable
without checking that it is fresh for t and s. Take x=(2,0), y=(3,0),
t=λy.x and s=λy.y. Both inputs have type 0→0. The default compTerm uses x as
its binder, so the intended free x becomes bound. The result has no free variables;
using genuinely fresh binder 4 instead preserves free x. iterTerm has the same
problem when its arbitrary endomorphism argument contains its chosen binder free.

UtilityCapture.lean proves these facts with kernel checking and no axioms. It
imports only the submitted definition, not any open target. The composition
counterexample is not a disproof of combinators_and_sufficiency: addComb, mulComb,
and constComb use safe binder/type combinations. The main characterization can
therefore still hold despite this general-purpose helper defect.

Repair: compute a fresh id above all variables of the inputs, or explicitly
require freshness at every general-purpose use. If retaining raw constructors,
rename/document them as such and prove the semantic composition/iteration lemmas
under explicit freshness hypotheses. A definition body change requires a replacement
published item and updated imports; documentation-only qualification may suffice
if all current safe specializations are deliberately retained.

Coverage and source correspondence:

1. Syntax, substitution, alpha/beta relations, numeral representation, definitions
   of polynomial/extended polynomial/piecewise polynomial, and subterm grammar:
   inspected in full. Typed variables are (id,type) pairs, so equal numeric ids at
   different types do not collide. Capture-avoiding substitution chooses ids above
   the body, substituted term, and target variable. Sequential substMany is safe
   in the target lemma because the substituted Church numerals are closed and Fs
   is injective. No opaque axiom or hidden main conclusion occurs in these classes.

2. combinators_and_sufficiency (4694e83f-7878-4185-8cdf-964a0d906b8a):
   Covers the four displayed source combinators and closure under composition.
   The numeral normal-form classification explicitly allows λalpha.alpha, which
   the paper's informal sentence overlooks under beta-only equality. This is an
   appropriate correction: it is beta-normal and eta-equivalent to numeral one,
   but is not alpha-equivalent to the expanded Church numeral. Do not remove this
   exception without changing the equality convention.

3. normal_subterm_structure (aadc9a8e-5141-4915-b574-db398c5d5716):
   Source p114 analyzes the binary case; the submitted k-variable generalization
   retains its type/free-variable hypotheses and matches clauses (1)–(3).
   The free-variable hypotheses are needed and are present. Bound ground-type
   variables in subterms are permitted by the grammar.

4. subterm_polynomial_lemma (8057498b-8e0a-4096-bdd4-ec2e8be75632):
   Positivity and distinct Fs are explicit. The exceptional alpha case accounts
   for beta-only reduction instead of silently eta-expanding alpha. The fixed
   terminal ground variable may be the bound variable or a free variable of s.
   No counterexample or lost source hypothesis was found in this review.

5. definable_iff_extended_polynomial (d41263bd-5699-47e6-9aea-b2d2af7f2deb):
   The headline is a genuine equivalence between independently defined syntactic
   definability and the two polynomial classes. It does not assume the desired
   characterization. Arity zero is meaningful and gives constants. Definition
   uses reduction to the expanded numeral; for this existence-of-a-representing-
   term characterization the numeral-one eta-short exception can be handled by
   choosing an expanded representative. The source's binary argument is generalized
   to k arguments, as the mission explicitly states.
